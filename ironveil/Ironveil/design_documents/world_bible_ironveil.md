# 🌍 THE WORLD OF IRONVEIL
### A World Bible for the Steampunk Rebuilder Project

---

## PART I: HISTORY & LORE

### The Old World
Before the war, the world of **Ironveil** was a marvel of advanced civilization. Humanity had mastered fusion energy, artificial intelligence, aeronautical engineering, and biomechanical augmentation. Great cities rose like crystal spires, connected by networks of rail lines and aerial shipping lanes. Autonomous machines tended farms, AI advisors guided governments, and zeppelins the size of ocean liners carried goods and passengers across continents.

It was an age of wonder. And like all ages of wonder, it bred complacency — and then ambition.

### The Sundering (The Great War)
Approximately **fifty years** before the events of the game, the world was consumed by a catastrophic global conflict known as **The Sundering**. The exact causes are debated — territorial disputes over rare Aetheric Ore deposits, ideological fractures between the great power blocs, or perhaps simply the inevitable result of too much power in too many hands.

What is not debated is the devastation.

The Sundering lasted **seven years**. It was fought with weapons that combined the most destructive elements of advanced technology: autonomous war machines, weaponized AI, orbital bombardment platforms, chemical defoliants, and massive armored zeppelins called **Dreadnoughts** that rained fire on cities below. Entire regions were rendered uninhabitable. The great cities — those gleaming spires — were shattered. Infrastructure collapsed. The AIs that once managed civilization were corrupted, destroyed, or went silent.

When the war finally ground to a halt — not through treaty, but through mutual exhaustion — the world was broken.

### The Quiet Years (Year 0 – Year 30)
The first three decades after The Sundering were called **The Quiet Years** — not because they were peaceful, but because the world had gone silent. Communications networks were down. Communities were isolated. Survival was the only priority.

During this time:
- Scattered communities formed around salvageable resources
- Knowledge of the Old World's technology began to fade as the engineers and scientists who built it died off
- **Marauder bands** formed from displaced soldiers, deserters, and desperate survivors who chose predation over cooperation
- A few visionary leaders began organizing **Reconstruction Guilds** — groups dedicated to understanding and rebuilding the old technology
- The natural world began to aggressively reclaim the ruins, creating haunting landscapes of crumbling towers wrapped in vines, forests growing through factory floors

### The Rekindling (Year 30 – Present, ~Year 50)
Approximately twenty years before the game begins, a period known as **The Rekindling** started. Key breakthroughs reignited hope:

- **Aetheric Ore** deposits were rediscovered — the rare crystalline mineral that powered Old World technology. Unlike fossil fuels, Aetheric Ore is clean, potent, and seemingly self-replenishing over long periods
- **Reconstruction Guilds** successfully reverse-engineered basic Old World machines, adapting them with cruder but functional steampunk-style engineering (brass, copper, steam, gears — because the precision manufacturing of the Old World was lost)
- Small **Beacon Towns** began to emerge — communities that restored power, established trade routes, and created zones of relative safety
- The player's home region became one of these emerging Beacon Towns

The world is now in a state of **cautious optimism**. The scars of The Sundering are everywhere — craters, ruins, dead zones, corroded war machines half-buried in fields — but flowers grow in the craters, children play in the shadows of broken towers, and the sound of hammers and engines fills the air where once there was only silence.

## PART II: GEOGRAPHY & REGIONS

### Continental Overview
The known world of Ironveil is divided into **one primary continent** called **Vantara** and several offshore island chains. The game world encompasses the central and most historically significant portion of Vantara.

### The Seven Regions of Vantara

#### 1. 🏔️ THE ASHSPINE MOUNTAINS (North)
A massive mountain range that forms the northern border of the known world. During The Sundering, several military installations were built deep within these mountains. Many are now sealed, collapsed, or occupied by marauders.

- **Terrain**: Jagged peaks, deep valleys, snow-covered passes, hidden caves
- **War Scars**: Collapsed tunnel networks, abandoned bunker complexes, crashed dreadnought wreckage embedded in mountainsides
- **Current State**: Largely unexplored. Rumors of functioning Old World facilities deep underground. Dangerous due to unstable terrain and marauder strongholds
- **Resources**: Rare metals, Aetheric Ore veins deep in the rock, salvageable military technology
- **Danger Level**: High

#### 2. 🌾 THE VERDANT BASIN (Central — Player's Region)
A wide, fertile valley nestled between the Ashspine Mountains to the north and the Rustwood to the south. This is where the player's home — **Coppervale** — is located. Once a major agricultural region, it was heavily bombed during The Sundering but has recovered remarkably well due to its natural fertility and access to clean water from mountain runoff.

- **Terrain**: Rolling hills, farmland (some still scarred), rivers, meadows, scattered ruins
- **War Scars**: Craters converted to ponds, rusted war machines used as planters and fences, a massive impact site called **The Hollow** at the valley's eastern edge
- **Current State**: The most recovered region. Coppervale is a thriving Beacon Town. Surrounding smaller settlements are growing. Trade routes connect to neighboring regions
- **Resources**: Fertile soil, clean water, moderate Aetheric Ore deposits, abundant salvage from surface ruins
- **Danger Level**: Low (within Coppervale), Moderate (outskirts and The Hollow)

#### 3. 🌲 THE RUSTWOOD (South)
A vast forest that has grown over and through the ruins of what was once the continent's largest industrial district. The trees here have an unusual reddish-copper tint to their bark and leaves — likely a result of mineral contamination from destroyed factories. The forest is beautiful but eerie.

- **Terrain**: Dense forest, overgrown factory ruins, rivers stained amber, fungal groves
- **War Scars**: Entire factory complexes consumed by forest growth, toxic pools, corroded pipelines
- **Current State**: Partially explored. Lumber and salvage operations exist at the edges, but the deep Rustwood is treacherous. Strange sounds echo from the ruins — possibly malfunctioning Old World machines still running on residual power
- **Resources**: Timber (unusual properties due to mineral absorption), salvageable industrial machinery, rare fungi with medicinal properties
- **Danger Level**: Moderate to High

#### 4. ⚓ THE SHATTERED COAST (West)
The western coastline, once home to the continent's greatest port cities. During The Sundering, massive naval battles and coastal bombardment reduced these cities to ruins. The coastline itself was altered — sections collapsed into the sea, creating a jagged, treacherous shoreline dotted with the hulks of destroyed warships.

- **Terrain**: Cliffs, rocky beaches, tidal ruins, sunken ship graveyards, sea caves
- **War Scars**: Destroyed port cities, half-sunken warships, coastal fortifications, an entire district that slid into the ocean (now visible at low tide)
- **Current State**: Fishing communities have established along safer sections. A few daring salvage operations recover materials from sunken ships. Pirates (maritime marauders) are a constant threat
- **Resources**: Seafood, naval salvage, Old World navigation and communication technology, Aetheric Ore from underwater deposits
- **Danger Level**: Moderate (coastal settlements), High (open water, deep ruins)

#### 5. 🏜️ THE SCORCHLANDS (East)
The eastern region bore the worst of The Sundering. Orbital bombardment and chemical weapons turned what was once a temperate region into a scarred wasteland. The ground is cracked and discolored, vegetation is sparse, and the air sometimes carries an acrid tang.

- **Terrain**: Cracked earth, impact craters, glass fields (sand fused by intense heat), sparse scrubland, dust storms
- **War Scars**: The most devastating — entire cities reduced to glass and ash, massive craters, contaminated zones
- **Current State**: Largely uninhabitable except along the western edge where it borders The Verdant Basin. A few hardened outposts exist, run by prospectors seeking Aetheric Ore (which is abundant here, pushed to the surface by bombardment). This is also the primary territory of the **Iron Marauders**, the most dangerous marauder faction
- **Resources**: Abundant Aetheric Ore, Old World military technology (dangerous to recover), rare minerals formed by extreme heat and pressure
- **Danger Level**: Very High

#### 6. 🏛️ THE SPIRE WASTES (Central-East)
The ruins of **Solara**, the Old World's greatest city. Once home to millions, Solara was the seat of government, culture, and technological achievement. Its iconic crystalline spires could be seen for miles. Now those spires are shattered stumps jutting from a landscape of rubble, overgrowth, and silence.

- **Terrain**: Urban ruins, collapsed skyscrapers, underground metro systems, overgrown parks and plazas
- **War Scars**: The city is the war scar. Entire districts leveled, others eerily intact but abandoned. The central government tower — **The Grand Spire** — still partially stands, a haunting silhouette on the horizon
- **Current State**: Considered sacred and dangerous in equal measure. Reconstruction Guilds mount expeditions here to recover Old World knowledge (data cores, blueprints, library archives). The ruins are also home to scavengers, marauders, and possibly still-active Old World defense systems
- **Resources**: Irreplaceable Old World knowledge and technology, architectural salvage, data cores containing pre-war information
- **Danger Level**: Very High

#### 7. 🌿 THE GREENREACH (Northwest)
A region that was relatively spared during The Sundering — not because it was defended, but because it was considered strategically unimportant. Rolling green hills, old-growth forests, and clean rivers make this the most "natural" region remaining.

- **Terrain**: Lush forests, rolling hills, clean rivers and lakes, old farmsteads
- **War Scars**: Minimal direct damage, but the region absorbed many refugees during and after the war, straining resources. Some areas show signs of overfarming and deforestation from the Quiet Years
- **Current State**: The breadbasket of the recovering world. Multiple thriving Beacon Towns. Strong agricultural output. Politically influential as food is power
- **Resources**: Agriculture, timber, livestock, clean water, limited salvage
- **Danger Level**: Low

## PART III: FACTIONS & GROUPS

### The Reconstruction Guilds
Organized groups dedicated to recovering and rebuilding Old World technology. They are the closest thing to a governing authority in most regions. Multiple guilds exist, sometimes cooperating, sometimes competing:

- **The Gearwrights' Guild**: Focused on mechanical engineering — engines, vehicles, construction machines. The player likely has ties to this guild
- **The Aether Society**: Specialists in Aetheric Ore and energy systems. Somewhat secretive and academic
- **The Skyfarers' League**: Dedicated to rebuilding aerial transportation — zeppelins, airships, flying machines. Romantic adventurers at heart
- **The Archive Corps**: Obsessed with recovering Old World knowledge. They mount dangerous expeditions to The Spire Wastes and other ruin sites

### The Marauders
Not a single unified force, but multiple bands of varying size, organization, and danger:

- **The Iron Marauders**: The largest and most dangerous faction. Based in The Scorchlands, they are the remnants of a Sundering-era military unit that never stood down. Disciplined, well-armed (by post-war standards), and territorial. They raid Beacon Towns for supplies and technology. Their leader is known only as **The Marshal**
- **The Rust Wolves**: A loose confederation of bandit groups operating in The Rustwood. Less organized than the Iron Marauders but more numerous. They prey on salvage teams and lumber operations
- **The Tide Reavers**: Maritime raiders along The Shattered Coast. They use salvaged warships and are a menace to fishing communities and trade vessels
- **Freelance Raiders**: Small, unaffiliated groups that operate anywhere. Opportunistic and unpredictable

### The Beacon Council
A fledgling alliance of the largest Beacon Towns, attempting to create a framework for governance, trade, and mutual defense. Still fragile and plagued by disagreements, but represents the best hope for lasting civilization.

### The Remnants
Mysterious figures and groups connected to the Old World. Some are descendants of pre-war leaders, scientists, or military officers who preserved knowledge in secret. Others claim to have access to still-functioning Old World AI systems. Their motives are unclear — some seem genuinely interested in helping rebuild, while others may have darker agendas.

## PART IV: TECHNOLOGY & RESOURCES

### Aetheric Ore
The cornerstone resource of both the Old World and the new. A crystalline mineral with remarkable energy-storage properties. When refined, it can power machines, weapons, AI systems, and more. It slowly regenerates over decades, making it technically renewable but scarce in the short term.

- **Raw Form**: Bluish-purple crystals found in underground deposits
- **Refined Form**: Glowing blue-white fuel cells and power cores
- **Uses**: Powers everything from small tools to massive zeppelins
- **Significance**: Control of Aetheric Ore deposits is the primary source of political and military power

### The Steampunk Aesthetic (In-World Explanation)
The Old World's technology was sleek, precise, and miniaturized — think clean lines, holographic interfaces, and molecular-scale engineering. That level of manufacturing precision was lost in The Sundering.

The rebuilding world uses **cruder but functional** engineering: brass fittings, copper piping, visible gears and pistons, steam-driven mechanisms, analog gauges and dials. This isn't a choice — it's necessity. The *principles* of Old World technology are understood, but the *tools* to build it with that precision no longer exist. So engineers adapt, improvise, and create something new — a hybrid of advanced knowledge and handcrafted construction.

This is why the world looks steampunk: **it's advanced technology filtered through artisanal manufacturing**.

### Key Technology Categories
1. **Mech Frames**: Bipedal or quadrupedal walking machines used for construction, defense, and heavy labor. Range from small utility frames to large combat walkers
2. **Zeppelins & Airships**: The primary form of long-distance transportation. Range from small personal craft to massive cargo haulers
3. **Automatons**: Robotic workers powered by Aetheric Ore and guided by simplified AI. Used for repetitive tasks, dangerous work, and as the player's "team"
4. **Aether Engines**: Power plants of various sizes that convert refined Aetheric Ore into usable energy
5. **Communication Arrays**: Rebuilt radio and signal networks. Still patchy but improving
6. **Defense Systems**: Walls, turrets, energy shields (rare and power-hungry), and armed mechs

## PART V: COPPERVALE — THE PLAYER'S HOME

### Overview
**Coppervale** is a Beacon Town located in the heart of **The Verdant Basin**. It gets its name from the copper-rich soil in the area and the distinctive copper patina on many of its rebuilt structures. It is a community of approximately **200 residents** — large enough to have a functioning economy and social life, small enough that everyone knows everyone.

### Layout
Coppervale is structured similarly to Mineral Town from Harvest Moon: Back to Nature:

- **Town Square**: The heart of the community. Features a rebuilt clocktower (the town's pride), a message board, and a gathering space for festivals
- **Market District**: Shops, a general store, a parts dealer, a food vendor, and a tavern called **The Rusty Gear**
- **Residential Area**: Homes for NPCs, ranging from modest repurposed structures to more fully rebuilt houses
- **The Workshop District**: Where the player's property is located — a large plot with a main workshop building, storage, and outdoor construction areas. This is the "farm"
- **The Overlook**: A hill at the north edge of town with a view of the Ashspine Mountains. Popular spot for quiet moments and, perhaps, romance
- **The Hollow Road**: The eastern path out of town that leads toward The Hollow — the massive impact crater that marks the edge of safe territory
- **The Rail Station**: A partially rebuilt rail platform. Currently non-functional but a major town goal is to restore rail service to other Beacon Towns

### Key NPCs (Examples)
- **Mayor Linden**: A weathered but optimistic leader who remembers The Quiet Years. Believes in Coppervale's future
- **Spark**: The town's young, enthusiastic mechanic and the player's first friend/rival. Dreams of building a flying machine
- **Old Maren**: An elderly woman who was an engineer in the Old World. Her knowledge is invaluable but her memory is fading
- **Captain Harrow**: Leader of the town's small militia. Gruff, protective, haunted by his past as a soldier during The Sundering
- **Pip**: A child who idolizes the player and collects "relics" (mostly junk, but occasionally something valuable)
- **Various merchants, farmers, crafters, and other residents** to flesh out the community

### The Player's Workshop
The player inherits (or is granted) a large, dilapidated property on the edge of town. It was once a maintenance depot for agricultural machines. The main structure is a large workshop building with:

- **Assembly Floor**: Where the player builds machines (mechs, zeppelins, automatons, etc.)
- **Forge**: For metalworking and component crafting
- **AI Core**: A damaged but recoverable Old World AI system that, once repaired, becomes the player's advisor and manages their automaton team
- **Storage Yard**: Outdoor area for storing materials, completed machines, and raw salvage
- **Living Quarters**: A small attached apartment — the player's home

### Seasonal Cycle & Daily Life
Following Harvest Moon's structure:
- **Seasons** affect available resources, NPC schedules, festivals, and marauder activity
- **Daily routine**: Wake up → manage automatons/assign tasks → work on builds → explore/salvage → socialize → sleep
- **Festivals**: Seasonal celebrations unique to post-war culture (Remembrance Day for the fallen, Spark Festival celebrating the first restored power grid, Harvest Faire for the agricultural yield, etc.)
- **Relationships**: Befriend NPCs through gifts, conversation, and completing tasks. Romance options available
- **Marauder Raids**: Periodic attacks that the player must prepare for by building defenses and combat machines

## PART VI: THEMES & TONE

### Core Emotional Arc
**"The world was broken. It's getting better. And you are why."**

The game begins with the player arriving in a community that has hope but lacks the means to fully realize it. Through the player's mechanical genius and determination, Coppervale grows from a struggling Beacon Town into a thriving center of innovation and safety.

### Visual Tone
Despite the post-apocalyptic setting, the visual style follows Harvest Moon: Back to Nature's **warm, charming aesthetic**:
- Soft, rounded character designs
- Warm color palettes (amber, copper, forest green, sky blue)
- Cozy interiors filled with handcrafted details
- War scars rendered as texture and character rather than horror — a crater becomes a beautiful pond, a destroyed tank becomes a flower planter, shattered glass becomes a mosaic walkway
- Weather and lighting that create atmosphere — golden sunsets through rusted structures, rain on copper rooftops, snow on broken spires in the distance

### Narrative Tone
- **Hopeful but honest**: The war was terrible and its effects are real, but people endure and rebuild
- **Community-focused**: Individual relationships matter as much as grand technology
- **Discovery-driven**: The Old World's secrets are everywhere, waiting to be found and understood
- **Player agency**: The player's choices and creations directly shape Coppervale's future
