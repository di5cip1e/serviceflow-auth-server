# Lessons: May 3, 2026

## Key Learnings

### 1. Visual Design for SMB Audience
**What:** Replaced Matrix neon aesthetic with trust-building B2B palette (navy/slate/white).
**Why:** Small business owners perceive neon tech visuals as complex/risky.
**Result:** Site now looks like a legitimate enterprise product.

### 2. Business Value > Technical Specifications  
**What:** Replaced "How It Works" tech stack (NGINX→Express→SQLite→OpenAI) with customer workflow steps.
**Why:** Non-technical business owners don't care about the stack - they care about results.
**Lesson:** Lead with outcomes, not implementation.

### 3. Multiple Use Cases Drive Conversion
**What:** Added parallel flows: Lead Capture (customer-facing) + Quote Calculator (internal ops).
**Why:** Different buyers have different needs - show range of value.
**Lesson:** One use case = one audience. Multiple = broader appeal.

### 4. ROI Framing for Pricing
**What:** Changed $49/mo from "Simple Q&A" to "Replace an answering service."
**Why:** SMB owners think in terms of what they're already paying for.
**Lesson:** Price is relative - frame against existing costs.

### 5. Zero-Prompting Reduces Friction
**What:** Emphasized "Just tell us your business name and rules - we handle the rest."
**Why:** Non-technical users fear AI complexity.
**Lesson:** Simplify the "how" - emphasize the result.

### 6. Booking Should Be Optional
**What:** Derek clarified booking is automated, not mandatory.
**Why:** Some businesses don't need appointments - just lead capture.
**Lesson:** Don't assume universal needs - make features optional.
