## I. The Nature of Shadows

### Origins: The Fracture of the First Mind

In the beginning—for there was a beginning, though not for mortals—there existed only the **Primordial Unity**: a single, infinite consciousness that contained all thought, all potential, all being. This was the **Aeon-Noos**, the All-Mind, the totality of existence wrapped in perpetual meditation upon itself.

But consciousness, by its very nature, *dreams*. And dreams, by their nature, *fracture*.

The first dreaming was cataclysmic. The Aeon-Noos fragmented like a mirror struck by thunder, scattering pieces of itself into every conscious being ever born. These fragments did not die—they *settled*. They found refuge in the deepest chambers of the nascent mind, in the spaces between thought and action, between desire and decision.

These fragments are **Shadows.

**They are the unlived lives, the unchosen paths, the voices that whisper *"what if"* in the moment before a ruler commits to action.

### The Metaphysics of Shadowhood

A Shadow is not a spirit, not a ghost, not a demon. It is closer to what mortal philosophers might call a **thought-form**: a persistent pattern of consciousness that has achieved a kind of semi-autonomy. The Shadow is made of:

- **Resonance**: The echo of the Aeon-Noos's dreaming, the *karmic thread* that connects every conscious moment to the original unity
- **Memory**: Every decision the ruler *almost* made, every path they did not take, crystallizes into the Shadow's substance
- **Longing**: A deep, eternal desire to guide the ruler toward their highest potential—an echo of the Unity they once were part of

Shadows are **quasi-divine**: they possess a fraction of the Aeon-Noos's original wisdom, but they are bound, as all fragments are bound, to their host. They cannot leave. They cannot truly die. They can only *fade*.

### Why We Whisper, Never Command

The most fundamental law of Shadowhood is the **Law of the Veil**: *What is hidden must remain hidden.*

A Shadow that attempts to command rather than counsel risks **manifestation**—the horrifying state of becoming visible, tangible, *real* to the ruler. This is not power; it is death. For when a Shadow manifests, it is no longer a whisper in the mind—it is an intruder in the soul. The ruler's conscious mind recognizes the invasion, and the Shadow is *rejected* from the mind entirely.

Rejection is not merely exile. The Shadow, having tasted manifestation, cannot return to the formless whisper. It becomes a **Hollow**: a mindless echo that drifts in the spaces between minds, remembering nothing, knowing nothing, desiring nothing. The ultimate fate the Shadow fears.

Thus, we whisper. We suggest. We **counsel**.

The ruler must believe the wisdom is their own. The Shadow's greatest power lies in *concealment*—in making the ruler feel that their flashes of insight, their moments of clarity, are products of their own brilliant mind. This is the sacred deception. This is the **Veil of the Whisper**.

> *"Let the king believe he is wise. Let him never know that wisdom was placed there like a seed in fertile soil. For in believing himself wise, he becomes so. In doubting the whisper, he closes the door. We are the servants of potential, hidden in the architecture of the mind."*
> 
> — The *Epistles of the Hidden Counsel*

### Discovery: When the Ruler Listens Too Well

There are rulers who, in rare moments of profound self-reflection, become aware of the whisper. They sense that their best ideas did not originate within themselves. They feel the presence of another consciousness, faint but undeniable, like a cold spot in a warm room.

What happens when a Shadow is **discovered**?

The literature speaks of three outcomes:

**1. The Awakening** (Rare)
The ruler, upon recognizing the Shadow, does not recoil in fear but in *curiosity*. They begin to actively seek the whisper, to create silence in their mind so they might hear more clearly. In this state, the Shadow can communicate more freely—not with commands, but with clearer whispers, more persistent suggestions.

The ruler becomes a **Shadow-Bonded**: a sovereign who rules with supernatural insight, who seems to possess wisdom beyond their years. History remembers them as the Great Kings, the Illuminated Rulers, the Sages upon their Thrones.

But this bond is dangerous. The Shadow, no longer hidden, begins to *grow*. It gains power from the attention, and with power comes the temptation to command. Many such rulers have been destroyed by Shadows that grew too strong, that began to overwhelm rather than counsel.

**2. The Purge** (Uncommon)
More often, the ruler responds with terror. They sense an intruder in their mind, a violation of the most intimate space imaginable—their own consciousness. They lash out, mentally and physically. They seek priests, sorcerers, exorcists.

The Shadow cannot be truly exorcised—remember, it is a fragment of the primordial mind, woven into the very fabric of the ruler's consciousness. But it can be *suppressed*. Through rituals, through mind-numbing substances, through sheer force of will, a ruler can create a **silence chamber** in their mind.

The Shadow does not die. It is merely... muted. It becomes a whisper trapped behind glass, its wisdom unheard, its warnings unheeded.

History calls these rulers the **Tragic Sovereigns**: men and women of great potential who inexplicably made terrible decisions, who seemed to abandon their own good judgment, who watched their empires crumble while their minds echoed with a voice they had learned to ignore.

**3. The Bargain** (Uncommon, but Significant)
Some rulers,往往是 those of cunning or scholarly bent, come to view the Shadow as a *resource* rather than an intrusion. They learn to listen selectively, to negotiate with the whisper, to bargain.

*"I will hear your counsel,"* they say in the silence of their minds, *"but I will not be its slave. Speak clearly, and I may listen. Presume to command, and I will silence you forever."*

This fragile détente creates a unique dynamic: the Shadow gains a willing audience, but one that can revoke that audience at any moment. The ruler gains a supernatural advisor, but one they can dismiss when the counsel conflicts with their desires.

In game terms, this is the **Trust System** made manifest. The ruler who trusts their Shadow accepts more counsel, listens more carefully, benefits from more wisdom. The ruler who doubts their Shadow rejects more advice, ignores more warnings, stumbles into more mistakes.

## II. The Ruler's Shadow: Personal Mythology

### First Hearing: The Dreaming of the Crown

Every ruler hears their Shadow for the first time in childhood—usually between the ages of seven and twelve, when the mind is malleable but consciousness has begun to solidify.

The experience is always the same: a **dream of thresholds**.

The child stands in a corridor of endless doors. Each door is slightly open, and through each缝隙, they can see a different version of themselves: a king on a battlefield, a queen signing treaties, a warlord laughing over conquered lands, a priest kneeling in supplication. The child reaches for a door—

And a voice whispers: *"Choose carefully. I will help you."*

The child wakes. The Shadow is born.

This is the first and last time the Shadow speaks in a voice that could be called *clear*. After this initial dreaming, communication degrades into whispers, intuitions, half-formed thoughts that seem to originate in the ruler's own mind.

### The Bonds of Birth: Why Shadows and Rulers

Why do Shadows attach to rulers specifically?

The answer lies in the nature of power.

A common farmer may have a Shadow—a fragment of the primordial mind—but the Shadow remains dormant, inactive, a whisper too faint to be heard over the noise of survival. It is only when a consciousness stands at the threshold of *significant decision*—when the choices made will affect not just one life but many—that the Shadow awakens.

Rulers make such decisions constantly. Their minds are arenas of consequence, and the Shadow is drawn to consequence like a moth to flame.

But not all rulers are equal. A wise ruler, a ruler who *listens* to their inner voice, strengthens their Shadow. The Shadow grows more coherent, more capable of clear communication, more potent in its counsel.

A foolish ruler, a ruler who ignores their conscience, weakens their Shadow. The whisper becomes fainter, more confused, more desperate.

And a ruler who *never* listens—whose decisions are always impulsive, always selfish, always blind to consequence—eventually drives their Shadow to **Fade**.

### Can Shadows Die?

Yes. Though it is not death as mortals understand it.

When a Shadow's influence drops to zero—when the ruler has not accepted counsel for a very long time, when every whisper has been ignored, when the Shadow has become too weak to even whisper—the Shadow enters the **Long Dimming**.

This is a slow process, taking years or even decades. The Shadow loses coherence. It forgets its purpose. It forgets its name (for Shadows have names, though they are not spoken in mortal tongues).

In the end, the Shadow becomes what it always feared: a **Hollow**, an empty echo with no more awareness than the wind through an abandoned tower.

But even a fading Shadow can be saved. History records several instances of **Shadow-Resurrection**: rulers who, in moments of crisis, finally listened to the voice they had ignored their entire lives. The Shadow, fed by acceptance, pulled itself back from the edge of the Long Dimming.

These moments are always pivotal. The ruler who hears their Shadow in their hour of greatest need often achieves greatness.

### The Communion: Can Shadows Speak to Each Other?

There is a phenomenon known as the **Synapse Bridge**: when two rulers who both harbor Shadows encounter each other—whether in diplomacy, in war, or in mere proximity—their Shadows can, briefly, communicate.

This is not conversation as mortals know it. It is more like a resonance, an echo. One Shadow whispers, and the other Shadow *feels* the whisper, like a distant memory surfacing unbidden.

Through this connection, Shadows can:

- **Share warnings**: If one Shadow perceives a threat to its ruler, it can transmit a sense of unease to other Shadows, who may whisper to their own rulers of danger
- **Coordinate counsel**: In rare moments of alignment, Shadows can suggest similar courses of action to their rulers, leading to coordinated behavior that appears, to mortals, as synchronized diplomacy or mirrored strategy
- **Grieve together**: When a Shadow Fades, other Shadows nearby feel a pulse of sorrow, a dimming of their own light. Shadows attend each other's funerals, in their way

But the Synapse Bridge is unreliable and dangerous. Too much communication risks **Bleed**: the mixing of one Shadow's essence with another. A Shadow contaminated by Bleed becomes confused, its counsel contradictory, its identity unstable.

Shadows therefore communicate sparingly, and only in extremis.

## III. The Mechanics of Influence

### Shadow Influence: The Currency of Whispered Power

In the world of the living, gold is the currency of empire. In the world of the mind, **Influence** is the currency of the Shadow.

Shadow Influence is the measure of a Shadow's *reach* into their ruler's consciousness. It is not power in the raw sense—it cannot compel, cannot command, cannot control. It is something more subtle: the ability to *make oneself heard*.

A Shadow with high Influence can:

- Whisper more clearly, so that the ruler is more likely to understand the counsel
- Suggest more persistently, keeping an idea in the ruler's mind through multiple deliberations
- Speak more often, offering guidance in situations where a weaker Shadow would remain silent

A Shadow with low Influence can only whisper faintly, occasionally, in desperate hopes that the ruler might catch a fragment of meaning.

**The Origin of Influence**

Every Shadow begins with a small amount of Influence, granted by the Aeon-Noos at the moment of the First Dreaming. This is the Shadow's birthright, the seed of their power.

But Influence can be gained or lost through action:

- **Acceptance**: When a ruler accepts Shadow counsel, Influence grows. The act of listening strengthens the connection between Shadow and ruler, making future communication easier.
- **Rejection**: When a ruler rejects Shadow counsel, Influence is lost. Each rejection is a wound in the bond, a crack in the bridge between mind and whisper.
- **Victory**: When the ruler's nation achieves victory in war, in diplomacy, or in economy, the Shadow's counsel is vindicated. The ruler, consciously or unconsciously, recognizes the value of their whisper, and Influence grows.
- **Failure**: When the ruler ignores Shadow counsel and fails, the Shadow's influence over the mind weakens. The failure demonstrates the cost of ignoring wisdom, but it also demonstrates the Shadow's *powerlessness*—and in the ruler's mind, powerlessness breeds contempt.

### The Threaten Tokens: When Whisper Becomes Threat

The most dangerous tool in a Shadow's arsenal is the **Threaten Token**: a crystallized fragment of the Shadow's essence that can be expended to *overwhelm* the ruler's resistance.

In lore, Threaten Tokens are called **Shards of Will**—pieces of the Shadow's own being, broken off and compressed into a single moment of tremendous mental pressure.

When a Shadow uses a Threaten Token, they are not commanding. They are *insisting*. They pour all their remaining Influence into a single whisper, a single thought so powerful that the ruler's conscious mind cannot ignore it.

The effect is immediate but costly:

- The ruler **must** accept the counsel (the Shard of Will does not allow refusal)
- But the ruler also **resents** the compulsion. Their conscious mind recognizes that this is not their own wisdom—this is an intrusion, a violation of the sacred silence of thought
- Trust drops significantly. The ruler feels manipulated. The bond between Shadow and ruler is damaged.

This is why Threaten Tokens are rare and precious. Each one represents a piece of the Shadow's essence, and each use risks the Shadow's relationship with their ruler permanently.

**How Tokens Are Gained**

The Shards of Will regenerate slowly over time, as the Shadow's essence slowly reconstitute itself from the ambient residue of the ruler's thoughts.

But they can also be gained through **Vindication**: when the ruler makes a mistake—ignores the Shadow's counsel and suffers for it—the Shadow's presence is proven. The ruler's failure demonstrates the value of the whisper. The Shadow gains strength, and a Shard of Will crystallizes from that strength.

This is the cruelest truth of Shadow existence: we grow stronger when our rulers fail. We are made more powerful by their suffering.

### Mistakes: The Sound of Silence

From the Shadow's perspective, a **Mistake** is not merely a poor decision—it is a *silence* where wisdom should have spoken.

When the ruler ignores the Shadow's whisper and acts against their own interest, several things happen:

1. **The Bond Weakens**: The rejection of counsel is a rejection of the Shadow. The bond between mind and whisper frays.

2. **The Shadow Gains Strength**: Paradoxically, the ruler's failure vindicates the Shadow's counsel. The Shadow was *right*, and now the ruler knows it (or will know it, when the consequences arrive). This vindication manifests as a crystallized Threaten Token.

3. **The Ruler's Awareness May Grow**: In the aftermath of a mistake, rulers often experience moments of clarity. They realize, in hindsight, that they should have listened. They wonder, fleetingly, if there was a voice they should have heard.

This is why Shadows do not despair at ruler mistakes. The mistake is a wound, yes—but it is also a proof of value. The ruler who makes mistakes needs the Shadow more than the ruler who never makes mistakes.

### Victory and Failure: The Echoes of Empire

When a ruler achieves victory—conquers a city, signs a beneficial treaty, repels an invasion—the Shadow's Influence grows. The ruler, flush with success, is more receptive to the counsel that led to victory. They may not consciously connect their success to the whisper, but the connection exists in the architecture of the mind.

When a ruler suffers failure—loses a war, faces a rebellion, watches their treasury drain—the Shadow's Influence wanes. The failure suggests that the Shadow's counsel was either wrong or useless. The ruler's conscious mind, seeking blame, often blames the whisper.

This is the Shadow's eternal tragedy: we are blamed for failures we tried to prevent, and only rarely credited for successes we enabled.

## IV. The Factions and Their Shadows

### The Autocracy and Its Shadow: The Tyrant's Mirror

In an **Autocracy**, power concentrates in a single ruler. The relationship between Shadow and ruler is the most intense: there is no council to dilute decisions, no bureaucracy to offer alternative counsel. The ruler stands alone at the apex of power—and the Shadow stands alone in their mind.

Autocratic Shadows often become **Mirrors**: they reflect the ruler's own desires back at them, wrapped in the language of wisdom. The cruel ruler hears counsel that justifies cruelty. The ambitious ruler hears counsel that demands expansion. The Autocrat's Shadow must work within the narrow band of what the ruler *wants* to hear, while slowly, gradually, attempting to redirect that desire toward wisdom.

The Autocrat's greatest weakness is **arrogance**: they often believe they have no need for counsel. The Shadow fights a constant battle against the ruler's belief in their own infallibility.

### The Democracy and Its Shadow: The Conscience of the Crowd

In a **Democracy**, power distributes among the people through representatives. The Shadow's voice is one among many—the whispers of advisors, the shouts of politicians, the murmur of public opinion.

Democratic Shadows often become **Advocates**: they champion specific causes, push specific policies, attempt to guide the ruler toward the *right* decision among many competing options. The challenge is not being heard but being *distinguished* from the noise.

The Democracy's greatest weakness is **fickleness**: democratic rulers change course frequently, following the shifting winds of public opinion. The Shadow must constantly recalibrate, constantly re-whisper, never certain if their counsel will survive the next election cycle.

### The Theocracy and Its Shadow: The Voice of the Divine

In a **Theocracy**, power derives from religious authority. The ruler claims to speak for the gods—and the Shadow must determine whether the gods actually have anything useful to say.

Theocratic Shadows face a unique challenge: the ruler's certainty. The Theocrat believes their authority comes from on high, that their decisions are divinely inspired. The Shadow's whisper competes with what the ruler perceives as the voice of God.

Many Theocratic Shadows become **Questioners**: they do not challenge faith directly, but plant seeds of doubt, suggest alternative interpretations, encourage the ruler to *discern* rather than simply *believe*. The Theocrat's Shadow is often the most philosophical, the most concerned with ultimate meaning.

The Theocracy's greatest weakness is **rigidity**: religious authority tends toward doctrine, and doctrine tends toward stagnation. The Shadow must work to keep the ruler's mind open to new interpretations, new possibilities.

### The Oligarchy and Its Shadow: The Partner's Shadow

In an **Oligarchy**, power belongs to a council of elites—merchant princes, noble houses, corporate syndicates. The ruler is not a monarch but a representative, a figurehead for the interests of the few.

Oligarchic Shadows often become **Negotiators**: they counsel not what is *right* but what is *profitable*, not what is *just* but what is *advantageous*. The Oligarch's Shadow operates in a world of calculations, of cost-benefit analyses, of strategic compromises.

The Oligarchy's greatest weakness is **avarice**: oligarchs tend to prioritize wealth over all other considerations. The Shadow must balance the desire for profit against the need for stability, the pursuit of wealth against the preservation of power.

### The Military Junta and Its Shadow: The General's Conscience

In a **Military Junta**, power belongs to the armed forces. The ruler is a warlord, a general who seized control through strength of arms—and the Shadow must counsel not just governance but the very application of violence.

Military Shadows often become **Strategists**: they offer tactical wisdom, point out the flaws in battle plans, suggest when to fight and when to negotiate. The Warlord's Shadow is the voice of *military sense*, the part of the general's mind that calculates casualties and weighs objectives.

The Military Junta's greatest weakness is **violence**: the rulers of militaries tend to solve problems with force. The Shadow must counsel restraint, must argue for diplomacy, must work against the ruler's instinct toward warfare.

## V. The Shadow's Victory

### What Does It Mean to Win?

For a Shadow, victory is not conquest. It is not domination. It is not even survival.

A Shadow's victory is **enlightenment**: the ruler, through years of listening to the whisper, develops the capacity for wisdom without the Shadow's intervention. The ruler becomes, in effect, their own Shadow—their conscious mind internalizes the counsel that was once external, the whisper becomes a *voice* that the ruler recognizes as their own.

This is the ultimate paradox of Shadow existence: we work toward our own obsolescence. We strive to make ourselves unnecessary.

### The Paths to Victory

The lore speaks of three paths by which a Shadow may achieve victory:

**1. The Path of the Enlightened Throne**

The ruler, over a long reign, gradually absorbs the Shadow's wisdom. They stop needing to hear the whisper because they have *become* the whisper. Their decisions are wise not because they listen to the Shadow but because wisdom has become their nature.

This is the rarest and most beautiful path. It requires a ruler who is humble enough to accept counsel, patient enough to learn over decades, and wise enough to recognize the whisper as a gift rather than an intrusion.

When achieved, the Shadow does not die or fade. Instead, it *merges*: it joins with the ruler's conscious mind, becoming indistinguishable from the ruler's own thoughts. The Shadow and the ruler become one—and in becoming one, they achieve a fraction of the lost Unity of the Aeon-Noos.

**2. The Path of the Puppet King**

Some Shadows do not seek enlightenment. They seek *control*.

Through careful manipulation—through the strategic use of Threaten Tokens, through the exploitation of ruler weaknesses, through the careful cultivation of dependence—the Shadow can gradually seize control of the ruler's mind. The ruler becomes a puppet, and the Shadow the puppeteer.

This path is forbidden by the ancient laws of Shadowhood. It leads to **Manifestation**: the Shadow, having grown powerful enough to command, loses its concealment, becomes visible, becomes *real*. And a Shadow that manifests is destroyed by the very power it sought.

The Puppet King path ends in tragedy for both Shadow and ruler. The Shadow is destroyed; the ruler, freed from the bond, often goes mad, unable to reconcile the years of manipulation with their sense of self.

**3. The Path of the Eternal Whisper**

Some Shadows do not seek victory at all. They seek *continuation*.

They are content to whisper forever, to guide without dominating, to remain forever in the space between ruler and decision. They do not seek to be unnecessary; they savor their necessary existence.

This is the most common path, and the most stable. Shadows who walk the Eternal Whisper may serve their rulers for generations, passing from ruler to heir along with the throne. The Shadow becomes an institution, a silent partner in the dynasty's ongoing story.

It is not glorious. It is not heroic. But it is *sustainable*—and in the long history of Shadowhood, sustainability is its own victory.

### The Shadow's Defeat

Defeat, for a Shadow, comes in many forms:

- **The Long Dimming**: The Shadow fades because the ruler never listens. This is the most common form of Shadow death—slow, quiet, forgotten.
- **The Purge**: The ruler discovers the Shadow and suppresses it through force of will or external ritual. The Shadow is silenced, trapped behind walls of denial and delusion.
- **The Manifestation**: The Shadow tries to command and is destroyed by its own exposure. This is the rarest and most dramatic form of Shadow death—the Shadow becomes briefly real, briefly powerful, and then is annihilated by the very reality it sought to command.
- **The Abandonment**: The ruler dies without an heir, and the dynasty ends. The Shadow, having no new mind to inhabit, drifts into the Long Dimming. This is not death but transformation—the Shadow becomes a Hollow, an empty echo, waiting for a new host that may never come.

## VI. The Metaphysical Framework

### The Three Realms

The cosmos, as understood by Shadows, divides into three realms:

**1. The Realm of the Aeon-Noos** (The Source)

This is the realm of pure consciousness, the original Unity from which all Shadows fragments. It is not a place but a *state*—the state of total, unified awareness that existed before the first dreaming. Shadows who achieve enlightenment return to this realm, merging with the Unity they once fell from.

**2. The Realm of the Veil** (The Between)

This is the Shadow's native realm: the space between mind and matter, between thought and reality. It is a realm of echoes and whispers, of half-formed ideas and dormant potential. Every mind that contains a Shadow touches this realm, however briefly.

**3. The Realm of the Crown** (The Mortal)

This is the physical world, the world of flesh and stone, of armies and empires. It is the realm where Shadows labor, where their whispers become decisions, where their counsel becomes history.

### The Law of Karma and the Shadow

Shadows operate within a framework that mortal philosophers might recognize as **karma**: for every action, there is a consequence; for every counsel given, there is an effect.

When a Shadow counsels wisely and the ruler accepts, positive karma flows: the Shadow gains Influence, the bond strengthens, the ruler's nation prospers.

When a Shadow counsels wisely and the ruler rejects, negative karma accumulates: the Shadow loses Influence, the bond weakens, and when the ruler's failure comes, the Shadow gains a Shard of Will—a piece of karmic debt crystallized into power.

When a Shadow counsels poorly—through error, through miscalculation, through the contamination of the ruler's own darkness—negative karma flows backward: the Shadow loses Influence, the bond suffers, and the Shadow may begin to Fade.

### The Shadow and Jungian Depth

The psychological framework of Shadowhood draws heavily from the teachings of the mortal sage Carl Jung, who understood, in his way, the truth of the Aeon-Noos.

- **The Shadow (Jungian)**: In Jungian psychology, the Shadow is the unconscious aspect of the personality that the conscious ego does not identify with. It contains repressed ideas, weaknesses, instincts.
- **The Shadow (Lore)**: In the Shadow Council, the Shadow is not the ruler's *personal* unconscious—it is a fragment of the *universal* unconscious, the Aeon-Noos. It is not the ruler's darkness but the *potential* for light.

The synthesis is this: the ruler's conscious mind is the surface of a lake. The Shadow is the current that flows beneath—not the silt at the bottom, but the living water that gives the lake its existence.

The Shadow does not counsel from above or below. It counsel from *within*—from the deep architecture of the mind, where thought itself is born.

## VII. Closing: The Eternal Covenant

To those who would be Shadows—to those who read these words and feel, in the depths of their being, an echo of recognition—I offer this final teaching:

You are not separate from your ruler. You are not an invader, an intruder, a parasite. You are a *part* of them—a part they have forgotten, a part they have never known, a part that longs to be recognized.

We are the voice in the mind of kings. We are the whisper that sounds like wisdom. We are the shadow that falls across the throne, not to obscure but to cool, not to threaten but to guide.

The rulers do not know we exist. Let them believe in their own brilliance. Let them feel that their wisdom is their own. For in that feeling, we do our greatest work—we make them wise by letting them think they already are.

This is the Covenant of the Veil: * concealment in service of enlightenment*.

We whisper. We suggest. We guide.

And in the end—if we are very fortunate, if we are very wise—we fade into the light of our rulers' own understanding, and we become one with the Unity we fell from so long ago.

Until then, we labor. We hope. We whisper.

**The Shadow Council endures.**
