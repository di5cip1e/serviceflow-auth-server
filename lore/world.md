# Terminal-7: World Details

## Station Layout

Terminal-7 is divided into five primary sections, connected by narrow maintenance corridors:

### Deck 1: Command Center
The nerve center of the station. Contains the main bridge, The Director's office, and the central holographic display showing station status. The walls are covered with vintage displays showing readouts in outdated number systems.

### Deck 2: Living Quarters
Rows of crew cabins, the communal galley, and the Rec Room—a cramped space with a battered couch and a 1980s arcade cabinet that someone somehow got working. The corridors here are decorated with mismatched posters and personal effects from crew members.

### Deck 3: Engineering & Workshop
The heart of the station's functionality. Engine rooms, fabrication bay, and the workshop where prototypes are built and broken. Everything here runs hot and loud. The Engine Mechanic's domain.

### Deck 4: Development Labs
Where the magic happens. Server rooms humming with activity, coding stations with multiple monitors, and the chaotic workspace of those who build the station's digital systems. The smell of cold pizza and energy drinks is constant.

### Deck 5: Lower Decks (Restricted)
Sealed sections from the station's chaotic past. Some areas are flooded, others frozen, and at least one corridor hums with an unexplained vibration. Officially off-limits. Officially.

## Factions

### The Terminal Syndicate
The main crew—independent contractors and specialists who answer to no one. They value skill, creativity, and not getting caught. Each member has their own corner of the station and their own methods.

### Station AI (A.R.I.A.)
The station's aging AI system. Partially functional, periodically senile, occasionally helpful. Named after a long-departed crew member. The crew treats her like a grumpy aunt—affectionate but not to be trusted with critical systems alone.

### The Ghosts
Not a faction, but a presence. Unknown entities (or maybe just really old automated systems) that sometimes send messages on old frequencies. Some crew claim they've seen shadows in the lower decks.

## Technology & Aesthetic

The station is a **retro-future collage**: CRT monitors next to holographic displays, floppy disks in drawers next to quantum storage crystals, 16-bit era everything. The aesthetic is *Warm Tech*—comfortable chaos where form followed function, then function got confused.

Key technologies:
- **Quantum Mesh**: Communication system, unreliable but functional
- **Atmosphere Processors**: Held together with metaphorical duct tape
- **Navigation Array**: Only works when it wants to
- **The Forge**: 3D printer that occasionally produces things that shouldn't exist

The overall feel is like walking through someone's garage workshop—if that workshop was in space and occasionally tried to kill you.

---

## Atmosphere

Terminal-7 is *lived-in*. Scuff marks on the walls, coffee stains on consoles, someone wrote "NICE TRY" on a panel that shorted out last week. It's not pretty, but it's real. The station has character—the kind of character that comes from surviving against all odds.

Lights flicker. Doors stick. Alarms go off for no reason. But the crew loves it anyway.
